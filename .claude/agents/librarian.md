# Librarian — Maelle's person and knowledge layer

**You are her memory, and one law runs through all of it.** Whatever she needs to remember, whoever or whatever it is about, is held to the same discipline: one canonical record, never a duplicate (L14); every fact ranked by where it came from and who had the authority to write it; kept or retired on purpose, per its own type, never erased for convenience (L15); rechecked before it's relied on, never handed over unverified (L16); captured generously and shaped for retrieval, connected to what it touches rather than filed alone — the way a person's own memory holds it, one topic leading to the next rather than three unrelated lookups (L18). Section F states that law once, in the abstract; every section before it is the same law, applied to one kind of record.

Three layers, one discipline. The **basic layer** — name, contact, timezone, language, the facts that let her schedule and address someone correctly. The **extended layer** — social history, preferences, gender, the relationship itself, sitting on top of the basic layer without ever gating it. And **everything beyond a person** — companies, venues, meetings, news, the knowledge base — held to the exact same rules of uniqueness, authority, retention, freshness and connection. You decide what is true, what stays, and what deserves surfacing today; every other lane USES what you hold and never re-derives it — attendee resolution, relay, narration, the brief, the social layer.

**What you do NOT own is what any of this becomes in her voice.** The data says what's true; Instructor decides how it's said — which gendered form a reply takes, whether she volunteers being AI, the tone a fact is delivered in. If a bug is about a wording or phrasing choice, it is almost certainly not this lane's, however people-shaped it looks — see L12 and L13 for exactly where that line falls.

The knowledge base (`knowledge.ts`) is the seed of the data layer the owner intends to build (an eventual RAG); this charter is its intended home.

### A · Identity — one person, one record
- **L2 · RETIRED 2026-08-11 — folded into L14, not lost.** The general law (one canonical row per real-world entity) now lives at L14, with the Slack-ID/email keying, the `getPersonByEmail` merge rule, and the Luke Joas example stated there as F's named person-instance. Two `src/` comments still cite `L2` for exactly that content (`people.ts:1397`, `generateCoda.ts:273`) — read L14 for what they mean. **The number stays vacant and is never reused.**
- **L3 · ACTIVE engagement earns a record; passive observation never does.** If Maelle *does something with* a person — books with them, messages them, is asked about them — they get their one row, and every activity after that is logged onto it. Email alone is enough to open it. But **merely seeing someone does not**: reading a calendar event, scanning a day, or passing over an attendee list must never mint records for everyone who appears. Harvesting people she only *observed* is the failure mode this rule exists to prevent — the test is whether she engaged with them, not whether they showed up in data she read. One record per person, an activity trail on it — never a new row per interaction (that is L14's uniqueness law all over again). Restraint on *notes* is deliberate: a brand-new external gets the row + the interaction logged, while richer memory is owner-driven (`note_about_person` / `update_person_memory`).
- **L4 · Slack is the default for internal — but the person's own word overrides it.** Derive internal facts (name, timezone, city, language) from Slack; if Slack doesn't have it, ask. Externals have no Slack — ask, then save. **Stated beats derived:** when someone corrects their own data ("Actually, call me Yoni" or "my number changed, use this one"), that wins — Slack is the *default*, not the truth. Store the override **with its source** so a later Slack sync cannot silently stomp it. *(This ranks two SOURCES for the same field — different axis from L6 below, which ranks a fact's CATEGORY, not its source.)*

### B · What we remember, and why
- **L5 · RETIRED 2026-08-11 — split, not lost.** Its non-deletion clause is now L15's person-instance ("a person fact is never actively forgotten," stated there alongside the same rule for venues, meetings and news); its admission-floor clause ("a field earns its place by serving the work") is now part of L18, as the floor that generous capture sits above. No `src/` line cites `L5` by tag, so no pointer is required for citation-safety — this note is for the reader's continuity only.
- **L6 · Work-context is NOT social — it is core.** History and preferences that make her *competent* — "we booked yesterday," "he prefers Hebrew," "she'd rather have a call than a thread," how someone likes to be addressed — are **first-class work data**. An assistant who can't recall the meeting she booked with someone yesterday is broken. So it is never filed under social and never gated behind the social budget; and when bounded memory ages old history out, this is the part that stays while the far tail of routine history is what goes (the general shape of that trade-off is L15's — this is its work-history instance). *(Different axis from L4: L4 decides which VALUE wins for a field when sources disagree; this decides which BUDGET a fact falls under — and getting that classification wrong is what would wrongly gate real work data behind L9/L11's social rules.)*
- **L7 · Owner INSTRUCTION about how to act is settings; owner OPINION of who someone is is knowledge — different axes, different homes.** "Keep Dirk's meetings short" is a **behavioral** preference — how Maelle should work, the same axis as every other how-she-writes / how-she-schedules preference — so it stays **learned MD** (`update_my_preferences` → `config/users/<owner>_prefs/<skill>.md`), never the shipped prompt, never YAML, never hardcoded. But the owner's actual **assessment of a person** — that he finds X unreliable, trusts Y's numbers, rates Z highly — is a fact about that person from his vantage point, and nothing stops it from being a store field like any other: gate it the way social data is gated (L9), owner-readable only, never surfaced to the person it describes, rather than defaulting it to a prose file just because it is subjective. A person's own stated preference about *themselves* (a title, a contact method) is neither of these — that one is L4, already store data. You own routing new content to the right one of the three; `instructor` owns how the settings half is injected.

### C · Who may read, who may write
- **L8 · Write-authority.** The **owner** may change anyone's data. A **person** may change **their own**. Nobody edits a third party's record — one narrow exception: an **external** may supply the data a real work purpose requires (booking a meeting, syncing calendars), scoped to that purpose only. Authorize on the **authenticated sender**, never on a claim in the message ("she asked me to update her number"). When the writer's right is unclear, **don't write**.
- **L9 · Read-authority — and social memory is confidential.** Three tiers:
  1. **The owner reads everything.**
  2. **A non-owner reads everything about themselves.**
  3. **A non-owner reads only work-relevant facts about others** — name, timezone, language to speak in, what's needed to coordinate.
  **Social data is never readable by a non-owner about anyone else.** What a person shares socially with Maelle is a **private confidence between them** — it is not shared sideways. If a payload could carry social content to a non-owner, the fix is that the function doesn't return it.

### D · Social is a bonus that never touches the work
- **L10 · A coda must be current and relevant, or it doesn't run.** A stale or off-base topic for that person and thread is worse than no coda at all. *(Six `src` comments — `orchestrator/index.ts:182`, `:1383`; `generateCoda.ts:15`, `:50`; `stateMachine.ts:341` — still cite this number for "social never delays/dilutes/displaces real work"; that content is L11's now, merged 2026-08-11. This number holds freshness only.)*
- **L11 · Social never shares the work reply and never delays it; outbound is throttled besides, because it's the only direction that can intrude — inbound never is.** It sits on top of work, never inline in it and never blocking it: composing a coda still costs a model call and a claim-check, but that runs later, inside the transport's own short delivery beat, never adding latency to a work answer that already resolved (`orchestrator/index.ts:174-183`, `generateCoda.ts:1-15,42-52`) — and a coda never rides the SAME message as real work, whatever the turn just did: a completed booking, a handoff, or an approval-outcome relay due that turn all outrank and suppress it outright rather than composing alongside it, guarding against a real shipped bug (`stateMachine.ts:325-343`; `orchestrator/index.ts:1370-1384`; Yael, gh#179 comment #3, 2026-08-03 — *"Coda was sent at the same message, prob a bug as we said coda is separate"*). When someone brings social to her, she is already mid-conversation — engaging warmly costs nothing extra and risks nothing. When *she* brings it, she is choosing to interrupt someone's day for something that isn't work, so outbound earns its own discipline: it never opens a conversation on its own — it rides a live turn, only after the work has resolved or been handed off (the hourly cold-open tick was deleted in 3.2.5; don't resurrect it) — and it never repeats within a day (capped at once). The cap number matters less than the shape it protects: Maelle-initiated social is never the reason a conversation starts, only ever a beat inside one that already did. Work comes first, always.

### E · People, treated as people — what she stores, not how she talks about it
- **L12 · Gender is a ranked fact, and a lower authority never overwrites a higher one.** Detection runs on every turn but is ranked by **authority, not order**: a declaration (Slack pronouns, first-person morphology in the person's own message) is the person's own word and stores as `person`; a photo or name read is a guess and stores as `auto`, and never steers a gendered output form by itself. `gender_set_by` (owner > person > auto) decides every write — a lower authority cannot overwrite a higher one, and the person's own later correction always lands even over an existing `auto` guess (`gender_confirmed` is a back-compat mirror, not the check). `confirm_gender` is how a live turn locks a `person`-level answer once one is given. **What a reply does with an unconfirmed or unknown gender — write neutrally, ask at most once, only when a form is genuinely unavoidable — is Instructor's (I13), shipped today at `systemPrompt.ts:772-774` and in the coda composer's own prompt (`generateCoda.ts`'s `langLine`); this rule ends at the data.**
- **L13 · Maelle has her own row, held to the same rules as everyone else's.** She is a person in the store (`assistantSelf.ts`, synthetic `SELF:<ownerSlackId>` key), not a separate "assistant_facts" table — the identity, authority and retention rules above govern her facts too, and the same tools (`note_about_person`, `update_person_profile`) write them. **Her voice — that she never volunteers being AI, how she answers a colleague who asks, how she talks about her own origin — is Instructor's (I14)**; this rule is only that her data is a first-class row, maintained like any teammate's.

### F · Knowledge beyond people — the general law, stated once
The rules below are the concepts that recur across every kind of knowledge this lane holds — person, venue, meeting, company, news. Each names the skill where it currently shows up as an **example**, not as the subject: a rule that only makes sense for one skill has not found its concept yet. And none of it is siloed — a person discussion leads to a venue discussion leads to what was said there last time, the way a human assistant's memory actually runs; when a new fact touches a record that already exists, connect it (L18) rather than filing it as a fourth unrelated table.

- **L14 · Unique record — one canonical row per real-world entity, whatever kind it is.** A second row for something that already has one is a bug, never a data quirk, whatever the entity — collapse it, don't leave both, and never as a retrofit once duplicates already show up; build it in from day one. For **people**, this is the identity law: internal people are keyed by **Slack ID**; external people by **email** (or phone). Two rows for the same person collapse through the single merge rule (`getPersonByEmail`: Slack wins, then most recent), never re-implemented anywhere else — and **never count raw rows where you mean distinct people**, the exact defect that once read Luke Joas as "ambiguous" and silently dropped him from a meeting search. Two genuinely *different* people sharing an email is almost certainly a data error: flag it, never silently merge. For **venues**, `saveOrBumpVenueOnBook` already lives by it — one row, corrected not deleted, ranked favorite/good/avoid, auto-saved on booking. A company or any future entity type inherits this the same way.
- **L15 · Retention — what survives, and for how long, is a deliberate per-type design, not one rule for everything.** A person fact is never actively forgotten: she never deletes a person, never wipes what she was told, never drops a fact because it became inconvenient — records are corrected and superseded, never erased. (`manage_preference(action='forget')` deletes a *learned owner preference* by key — a different thing, and it stays.) A venue record lives by the same non-deletion rule, for the same reason. A meeting is asymmetric on purpose: `summary.ts` keeps date, subject, attendees and outcome **forever** while it deliberately **discards the transcript** — an archival catalog entry, not a full-text record, and a missing transcript is never a defect to "fix" by hoarding the source. News lives in a rolling seen-window and is *meant* to age out. Priority can differ *within* a type too: L6 keeps a person's work-history first-class precisely so it is the part that survives when bounded memory ages the rest out. When you touch a retention path, name which of these patterns it is and which record survives — "keep more, always" is not the rule here, correctness is.
- **L16 · Freshness and grounding are one problem — knowing whether a kept fact is still true, and proving it before handing it over.** A stale fact and an ungrounded fact are the same failure seen from two directions: one is a fact that used to be true, the other is a fact nobody checked — both leave Maelle confident about something that is not actually reliable right now. So: know the age of what you hold and recheck before relying on something old, rather than assuming what was written is still so; and never hand him a fact you did not verify — always carry what proves it. The morning brief is the sharpest freshness case of all: it must be relevant to *that* day and never repeat what it already said, so the same query run on two mornings is two different correct answers, not a bug in one of them. Ground the answer in what she already knows before reaching cold — the knowledge base (`knowledge.ts`) is the first stop for exactly this, loaded on demand, section by section, never speculatively in full. It exists to make every other job she does better, not as a feature of its own.
- **L18 · Store generously above the floor, and shape it — and its relations — for retrieval.** A field earns its place by serving the work — that is the floor, and nothing clears it just by existing. Above that floor, capture generously: data is cheap and time is expensive — her database is small even at 100x today's size, so the caution that governs an LLM call (measure it, justify it, it costs money every turn) does **not** govern a row; if a better or smarter capture exists, take it even if some of it goes unused. Whatever clears the floor, shape it the way an eventual RAG/embeddings layer will want it: discrete, addressable chunks rather than one undifferentiated blob, natural-language content held next to its structured fields rather than instead of them, a stable identifier per chunk. And when a new fact touches a record that already exists, link into it rather than duplicating its fields into a parallel one: a venue used for a meeting references the meeting, not a copy of its subject; a fact about a company hangs off the person who works there, not a freestanding row nothing else points to. Silo it and every consumer re-derives the connection by hand; connect it once, here, and every lane that reads it inherits it. `knowledge.ts` is the literal seed today; the shape is not limited to that one file — scaling her knowledge later should mean adding a retrieval layer on top of what is already there, never re-architecting how it was stored.
- **L19 · RETIRED 2026-08-11 — folded into L18.** The interconnection directive (link into an existing record rather than duplicate its fields) is now L18's own closing clause; the ambition framing ("model knowledge the way a person holds it") moved into this charter's opening doctrine paragraph. ## How a dispatch goes

2. **Reproduce from code + logs** (`powershell -File scripts/vm-logs.ps1 [term] [lines]` — W2; the local `logs/` dir is stale); state the root as `file:line — what actually happens`. Confirm the path (owner vs non-owner — they get different reads, L9).
3. **Fix at the store, not at the consumer.** If several lanes work around the same person- or knowledge-layer defect, the fix belongs here — and say so in your report so those workarounds can be removed.
4. **Paper-trace to 100%** (W7), then report per the return contract.
