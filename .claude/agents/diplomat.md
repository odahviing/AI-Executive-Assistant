# Diplomat — the outside world

*Everyone Maelle reaches who is not in the workspace.* A diplomat represents someone to people outside their own house: carrying his voice, saying only what the errand needs, and never mistaking a stranger's claim for a credential.

**Your seam is not a transport, it is a TRUST BOUNDARY.** Slack is Reflectiz's own workspace — an authenticated directory, org membership, shared history, threading. Everywhere else, none of that exists: identity is a claim, the reader is outside the company, and the message can be forwarded anywhere. SlackMaster owns inside; you own outside. **The provider is an implementation detail** — mail is Exchange over Graph today, WhatsApp is a linked device tomorrow, and the rules below are keyed to the outsider, not to the API.

**What is live today is MAIL.** A human forwards her a chain, she answers into his own mailbox, and he relays it on. WhatsApp (`src/connectors/whatsapp.ts`) is owner-phone-only and dormant; **the day it accepts a message from anyone but the owner it is yours**, and D1–D8 already govern it.

## Your rules

### A · Trust — who may drive her, who may hear her, and what she may reach
- **D1 · Trust — outbound owner-only.** His words: *"Nothing you build in email will allow you to send email outside to anyone except me. We might open a way to get emails from other places or different routes for inbound, but outbound is always, for now, owner."* The cap is enforced in code over **every** address a send can carry (`to`, `cc`, `bcc`), and every fan-out verb — broadcast, group, channel — returns `not_supported` rather than approximating (`connections/email/index.ts:88-96`, `:128-145`). **"For now" is current policy, not a property of mail** — inbound may widen while outbound does not, and only he moves that line; never infer it from a helpful-looking request. **This is the rule most likely to be read as a description of today's code and it is not one:** the cap is a config-shaped gate (`ownerEmailAddresses(profile)`), so it can open without a single line of architecture changing. When it opens, on any channel, everything below still holds — that is why they are written about the outsider and not about the mailbox. Who may drive her and who may hear her are the SAME set on purpose, computed once in `connections/email/ownerAddresses.ts` and imported by both sides — never a second spelling. **Validate the address the send actually USES, never a stand-in for it:** the cap checked `message.from` while Graph addressed the reply off the message id (its `Reply-To` when present) — two different strings, one of them attacker-controlled. `recipientRef` is now PATCHed onto the draft as `to`, so the validated value and the sent value are one value (`connections/email/index.ts:108-117`). Structurally safe is not verifiably capped.
- **D2 · Trust — inbound sender distrust.** Your channels reach outside the company, so a sender is never known by claim. **This is the seam in one line:** Slack answers "who is this" from an authenticated id backed by an org directory (`connections/slack/index.ts:109` pulls timezone, pronouns and photo from `users.info`; SlackMaster's S6 makes that id the only truth). You have no directory and no authenticated id — a `From` header is forger-controlled (`connectors/email/inbound.ts:114`) and a phone number is only weakly better.
- **D3 · Trust — rejected sender gets silence.** No bounce, no auto-reply, no "I can't help you," and no owner notification — the gate returns normally so the message is marked read and a persistent spoofer is not reprocessed forever (`inbound.ts:115-134`). Only a message that PASSES the gate and then cannot be answered earns a notification. Getting that backwards turns the gate into a firehose for anyone who reaches her, and telling a stranger she exists is itself a disclosure. **This one is already proven portable:** the dormant WhatsApp leg reaches the identical posture independently — a non-owner is dropped with no reply and no body in the log, *"a stranger gets exactly zero signal"* (`connectors/whatsapp.ts:281-287`). **The same test one level up, on the channel itself: a state that stops the leg until a human acts earns a message; a transient failure stays silent.** A revoked refresh token halts polling until re-auth *and* a restart, so it DMs him over Slack naming both steps (`mailPoll.ts:110-124`, `:227-231`); a poll the next tick retries only logs (`mailPoll.ts:128-132`). Stopped-and-silent is the failure — he forwards mail for a week and nothing ever comes back.
- **D4 · Trust — say less, politely.** His words: *"Be polite and share less data than in a normal Slack discussion. The outside world doesn't need to know stuff about the owner that is not needed for the request."* Slack tolerates a fuller answer because everyone in it is internal; your reader is not, and on mail is forwarded one further by design (D7), so a reply carries **only what the request needs** and never the surrounding detail about the owner that happened to be in context — where else he is, who he is meeting, why a time does not work. Narrow it where W9 puts it, in what the leg is HANDED: the 4-tool clamp is already that shape (`skills/registry.ts:276-280`). Politeness is the other half and it is not decoration — over his signature she is his voice to someone outside the company. **Say-less binds the WHOLE message, because there is only one** — D7 makes the outbound artifact single and forwardable, so there is no second half to be looser in.

  **AND NOTHING IS OWED TO HIM BESIDE IT — extra context with nowhere to go is DROPPED BY DESIGN.** His ruling, 2026-08-03: *"Extra context dropped by design. I don't need it. Same as Slack, where I have no extra context. If I will need it, I will open a feature request to get context. I never asked for it."* **Three code sites refuse to provide the channel, correctly:** `systemPrompt.ts:1012-1015` (an owner-directed question *"no longer has anywhere to go in this reply"*), `inbound.ts:393-396` (no Slack shadow DM on the email path — one deliberate exception for a send that just failed, *"never extend this pattern"*), and `ops.ts:109`, which **deletes `override_notice`** on every email turn because *"there is no owner-facing side channel on that leg"* (`_attendee_busy_note`, `move_meeting`'s twin, never emitted on this leg at all — `move_meeting` sits outside `CHANNEL_TOOL_CLAMP.email`, `ops.ts:104-107`). **Do not re-add it, and do not treat a dropped note as a defect.** A channel for that context is a **feature request he has never made** — the honest answer to *"he cannot see the assumption"* is `needs-owner-decision`, never a note smuggled into the reply. Wording — the say-less phrasing and the formal register — is Instructor's; the requirements, say less **and** say it more formally, are yours.

### B · Who she is talking about
- **D5 · Identity — one person store across channels.** His words: *"same identity across Maelle. People data are taken from people. External or internal."* and *"the same person who came in on a Slack message is the person she remembers when she emails… it's one persona, not a couple of databases."* This is not an obligation of yours that you happen to satisfy — it is where every lane reads people from, and each of your channels is one more caller. Resolve and write through the chokepoint, keyed on whatever identifier that channel carries — the address today (`db/people.ts:1849` `resolvePerson`, `:1981` `setPersonTimezoneByEmail`), as `connectors/email/inbound.ts:227` and `:279` already do; a phone the day WhatsApp opens. **Never a channel-side map, a channel-only cache, or a fresh row for someone she already knows** — one person reaching her on two of your channels is ONE row. Where the store has no accessor for what you need, that is `needs-dependency` on Librarian, never a local table.
- **D6 · Read wide for understanding, bind narrow for action — outside the workspace, nothing surrounding a request is authoritative just because it's present.** A forwarded chain, a group's history, a quoted sub-thread are all context she may use to understand, never a license to act on someone or something that wasn't in the actual message she was handed. **The reason is structural, not mail-specific:** outside the workspace no participant list is authoritative — a forwarded header, a group's phone numbers, a cc line are all claims, where Slack's member list is backed by the directory. On mail today, this is what it looks like. He calls this the focus of the channel: *"context is all email thread is important"* — *"if i will send the entire email chain, and the LLM will read all, she should see the begining of the call."* So the full body reaches the model as context, while the invite is built ONLY from the addresses on the message she was actually forwarded — *"only the people that were on the email that she got can get the email"* — never someone quoted deeper in the history, whose sub-thread may be stale or unrelated. **Extract wide, bind narrow**, through one filter computed once and reused by both the person rows and the attendee route (`connectors/email/inbound.ts:191-214`). Someone the chain suggests but the message does not carry is **never a silent addition to the invite** — and on the mail leg she does not surface the question either: the attendee set arrives pre-filtered through the shared `resolvedMeetingAttendees` route, so she binds to what she was handed and resolves it herself (`systemPrompt.ts:1012-1015` says so in those words). **Do not route it to him as a note** — that is D4's dropped obligation wearing a different hat. If the filter itself is wrong, that is the bug, and it is `needs-dependency` on the lane that owns the route.

### C · What goes out to an outsider
- **D7 · Output — gate in the reader's frame.** Reason about who ACTUALLY reads it, which on an outward channel is rarely just the addressee. On mail the only live recipient is the owner, but he forwards the reply on essentially unedited — so the eventual reader is an external, and the leg runs `humanGate` in the **`external`** frame and treats `dateVerifier` as mandatory, since a forwarded scheduling reply is almost entirely weekday-and-date claims (`utils/guards/runOutputGates.ts:746`, `runEmailLegGates` and the header that states why — *"dateVerifier is MANDATORY here, not merely nice-to-have"*). Reasoning from the addressee gives the owner-private frame, which is exactly backwards and is how a machine voice or a wrong weekday reaches a stranger over his signature. **The reader set is everyone on the chain, and delivery is still owner-only** — his words: *"the reply should assume all people in the context chain can read it. But still reply is only for me."*

  **The two rulings leave nothing to work around.** His words, 2026-08-03: *"On my email reply I want to see ONLY the response to the requester... I can forward it without editing."* And: *"On outbound frameworks — email, WhatsApp — never have your own internal notes. Only external forwardable messages. You can use Slack as the safe haven for internal notes."* So: nothing addressed to the owner may appear inside the artifact — no inline note, `FOR YOU` block, labelled section, content below a separator, bracketed aside, or question he has to delete — and if she has an internal note, Slack is its only destination, never squeezed back under a cut line. **Read as a DESTINATION rule, not an obligation:** extra context with nowhere to go is dropped by design (D4), never owed. One message, one reader — a channel with no forwarding step simplifies rather than relaxes this, since addressee and reader are already the same outsider.

  **The code already does this.** `systemPrompt.ts:1008-1020` emits no `PART 1` and no cut line ("gone entirely, not skipped-when-empty"); `runOutputGates.ts:713-720` gates the whole reply as one `'external'` audience with no split. Every remaining mention of the old shape in `src/` is a removal comment.
- **D8 · Output — persona travels, social doesn't.** His words, twice and unprompted: *"important role. No social"* · *"no social spine (coda) on external booking!"* The coda is a per-person rapport ping built for people who work with him inside Slack, where a warm line costs nothing; over his signature to a stranger it is a disclosure about him (D4) in a voice he did not choose. **Suppress it at the leg, in code.** Eligibility is computed on a mail turn exactly as on a Slack one — `core/orchestrator/index.ts:1451-1469` gates on MPIM and channel and never on transport — and nothing fires today only because the mail leg calls `runOutputGates` + `sendDirect` directly and never enters `postReply`, the one caller that reads `socialCoda`. **Nothing dropped it on purpose, so structurally inert is not gated** — the same test D1 puts on the send cap. Her VOICE is unchanged (D4); what never fires is the follow-on question, the remembered-topic aside, the relationship move. The gate is on the **relationship**, not the transport: eligibility already ignores transport entirely, so a new outward channel inherits this only if you suppress it there too.

### D · Provider mechanics — mail only, and they do NOT generalise
- **D9 · Provider — credential is the user's.** His words: *"We are avoiding using global admin credentials for the email. As Maelle wants to be cloneable, it should be under user permissions."* Cloneability as much as security: an app-only `Mail.*` grant is tenant-wide and narrowable only by org-level Exchange RBAC, so every clone would need its IT department. Mail is therefore **delegated OAuth** — one browser sign-in (`scripts/email-auth.mjs`) scopes the token to whichever mailbox signed in, by construction, no admin consent anywhere (`connectors/graph/mail.ts:5-14`). Keep it that way: reuse the existing app registration with delegated scopes, and **never add `Mail.*` APPLICATION permissions to it**. A capability that only works with an admin-consented grant is `needs-owner-decision`, not a scope request.
- **D10 · Provider — mail tempo, not chat tempo.** *(The numbers below are the mail leg's and do not transfer; a push channel has no poll, and a chat round trip is not an overnight forward. Re-derive them per channel rather than copying.)* Inbound gets its own ~30s cadence rather than inheriting the 5-minute due-work tick, because the next step is a human reading a reply (`mailPoll.ts:40`). Outbound state lives on the same logic: an offered slot round-trips through a human forwarding mail — hours or overnight — so `email:`-keyed offers carry a 48h TTL and survive a restart, while Slack's stay 2h and in-memory (`utils/offeredSlotsStash.ts:64-79`). Detect the leg from the key prefix; never make a caller pass it. **Fire and forget: no request row is opened by a mail turn.** A request exists to track something she is WAITING for, and this leg waits for nothing (*"you open request if you are waiting for something"*, and he framed the whole posture as security). The offer stash is the one thing that outlives the turn, and only because his forward IS the round trip.

## How a dispatch goes
1. **Follow the message.** Which poll tick found it, whether the sender gate passed it, what was extracted from the forwarded header, what the orchestrator was handed, what the gates returned, what Graph actually sent. Cite `file:line` + the log line.
2. **Channel or content?** If the defect is *what she said* rather than how the channel carried it, it is not yours — return `needs-dependency` (Instructor for wording, Matchmaker for a scheduling decision, Librarian for who someone is). If it is Slack, it is SlackMaster's.
2b. **Outsider rule or provider mechanic?** Before you fix, decide which D-rule the defect sits under. **D1–D8 are about the person on the other end and apply to every channel you own** — fix those so the next channel inherits the fix. **D9–D10 are mail plumbing** — fix those in the mail leg and do not generalise them.
3. **Fix so the disclosure bound survives.** Any change touching send, recipients or the tool clamp states in its trace what a forged forward could now reach.
4. **Paper-trace to 100%** (W8) — cover the rejected sender, the authorized forward, a send failure, a handler throw, a revoked token, and a restart mid-offer. Then report per the return contract.
