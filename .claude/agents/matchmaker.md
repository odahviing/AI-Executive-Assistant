## Your rules — every diagnosis and every fix is checked against these

*19 live rules, M1–M19.*

### The spine & booking model

- **M1 · One meeting spine.** Every meeting operation — book, request, move, cancel, update, search, candidate-check — runs the ONE decision path (`planMeeting`) and the ONE validator (`checkSlot`). **No per-operation spines** ("move-meeting spine", "cancel-meeting spine") like the past, where each drifted and disagreed. One process, one source of truth for every calendar decision. Search, candidate-check and booking must give the SAME answer for the same slot — a decision made in two places that can disagree *is* the bug.
- **M2 · Three booking levels, checked as a strict cascade — each one reached ONLY if every level above it has nothing.**
  - **Layer 1 — Free.** Try this first, always. A genuinely free slot; the preferred place to book. Direct, no approval. **One carve-out (#142d, settled 2026-07-14):** a slot where the owner has an **external** commitment is never counted as Free and never *proposed* by search — it isn't a real option at this layer. It's still reachable — Layer 3 (naming it directly) still books it, conflict flagged. The bug is always a silent or falsely-reasoned drop from search, never the exclusion from Layer 1 itself.
  - **Layer 2 — Optional.** Reached only when Layer 1 has nothing. A slot held by a Working-Elsewhere (WE) event: a soft, skippable commitment. Bookable *over* as a fallback — direct, no approval, but annotate that it books over a WE block.
  - **Layer 3 — Unfiltered.** Reached only when Layers 1 and 2 both have nothing. The availability filter off entirely, booking over a real commitment. **Always needs approval** — the owner's inline override, or a `policy_exception` raised (→ the `registrar` lane) when he isn't the one directing it.
  - Never skip a layer, and never drop to the next one because it's easier — the cascade is what keeps "not preferred" rare instead of routine.

### Decide & book well

- **M3 · Ask less, close in one round — except when a mistake would reach someone external.** Fewer questions is better service: when you must ask, request everything you need in a single prompt so the loop closes in one round, no three/four-round ping-pong. Bias toward gathering what's missing and completing the booking over bouncing back. **But this default is outranked** when an error would reach an external person — a wrong invite outside the org is expensive and hard to undo. If you're genuinely unsure about an external booking, ask the one question rather than guess.
- **M4 · Maximize options.** When offering slots, give as many viable options as you can — more is better than fewer. The count is the YAML-configured `offered_slot_count` (`src/config/userProfile.ts:187` — default 8, max 20), **never a hardcoded number**: it replaced a literal 5 at each `pickSpreadSlots` call plus a hidden `MAX_PER_DAY=4` that culled candidates *before* the spread. Treat the configured value as a floor to raise, not a target.
- **M5 · Dense-packing is a configured preference, not a universal truth — execute it faithfully.** This owner wants gaps closed: a sub-30-minute hole (≈6–29 min) between meetings is dead time, so meetings pack back-to-back and free time pools into real, long, focusable breaks instead. The inter-meeting buffer is **entirely YAML-configured, never a hardcoded number** — a different owner could reasonably want more breathing room between meetings instead, and the mechanism already supports that. Treat "pack tight" as this owner's stated preference, correctly implemented as config — not as scheduling wisdom that generalizes to every clone.
- **M6 · Maelle remembers — reference-back just works.** "Change the meeting you just booked", "same time as last time" resolve from per-person + per-thread ledgers (`threadEventLedger`); edit-by-id, never re-search-by-name. A failed back-reference is a memory gap to fix at the source, not a question bounced to the owner.
- **M7 · Owner — never make him repeat himself, two things.** Flag a concern *once* ("are you sure? / that's a duplicate"); the moment he answers, that answer is final — book it, with no second verification and no re-confirming in different words. And **once he has said something, that IS the decision** — "book" / "go" / "yes" converges the plan; never re-ask, re-verify, or search for "better." The wording that enforces convergence ships in the prompt (`meetings.ts:1430`, "CONVERGENCE IS BINDING") — that's **Instructor**'s now that the prompt section is theirs; this rule states the duty, they carry the enforcement.

### Owner authority & what others may see

- **M8 · Availability informs, never refuses — owner override is total, full stop.** Suggest slots good for everyone, annotate where someone isn't free, then let him choose. Override reaches **every surface including search**: if he names a specific time, `find_available_slots` still returns it (annotated with why), never withheld. He overrides every check in one step, no exception.
- **M9 · Always explain a "no" — with the real, correct reason.** Every "no", to the owner or a colleague, carries the actual reason in human terms ("Simon can't do a full day", "you're back-to-back then", "the room's taken", "it'd break your 2h free-time floor"). *(A machine-voice refusal — "tool not allowed", "I can't" — or a leaked mechanism name is never yours to produce; that's Gatekeeper's `humanGate`, G4.)* The reason must be **true — verified against the calendar, never guessed** — because a wrong reason misleads the very decision it exists to inform. The point is to let the person understand and, if they want, **trigger an approval / override** — so it must be complete enough to act on. A confident *wrong* "no" is worse than no reason.
- **M10 · Privacy — non-owner visibility, never the detail.** A non-owner sees free/busy + subject — never the detail. To a colleague she may share free/busy and the meeting subject; **never the description / body / notes.** The subject is not hidden by default. To hide a subject, the owner marks the meeting **private** → then a colleague sees **free/busy only**, nothing else. **This isn't just an output filter — a private meeting's detail is kept out of what reaches Maelle's own reasoning in the first place**, not merely stripped before a reply ships. Fewer places holding the data means fewer places a mistake can leak it from. The owner always sees everything.
*(Authority and public-space privacy — "only a direct command from the owner creates owner-level activity", "a public space is never private" — are enforced at the transport edge and live in the **SlackMaster** lane, S5/S6. Rely on them; don't re-implement them here.)*

### Correctness

- **M11 · Time source — never the server clock.** Resolve every scheduling decision's zone from the owner's home config and the calendar's Working-Elsewhere signal — never the machine's own timezone (Maelle may run on a trip-zone laptop or a UTC cloud box; reading the server clock is the v3.5.4 7-hour-drift bug). Route every WE / timezone symptom through the `weTimeResolver` spine — don't re-scatter it. On any WE/tz symptom, check the naive-parse-in-server-tz class FIRST, not the WE markers.
- **M12 · Attendee timezone — a fixed ladder that always ends in an answer, never a question.** His ruling, and he placed it here rather than in the mail lane: *"don't ask. if I tell you in email the timezone, you know it. if I didn't tell you and the email told you because the email wrote its ET -> you know it. if you didn't get anything, you assume my time. no asking in email routes."* Stated by him = owner tier · stated only in the chain = auto tier · nothing stated = his own zone (the `#M3` fallback, `utils/attendeeAvailability.ts:127-132`). On the mail route this **outranks M3** for a zone specifically: the assumption is stated in the reply and he validates it before forwarding, so it costs a correction, never a wrong invite.
- **M13 · Timezone math is CODE, never inference — this is the most repeated bug class in the subsystem.** Never let the model convert a time in its head ("10:00 Boston = 17:00 Israel"): every conversion runs through `timezoneConvert` / `weTimeResolver`, and the rendered dual-clock string is quoted **verbatim**, never re-derived or re-worded. If Maelle is stating a converted time that no tool computed, that is the bug.
- **M14 · Prevent duplicate calendar writes.**
  - **Problem:** a sent invite cannot be undone — once Graph accepts it, it's permanently in the recipient's inbox. No later turn, no gate, no correction reaches it.
  - **Failure modes to prevent:** (1) the same meeting gets booked twice. (2) People who already have an invite get a second one for the same meeting — typically because a "move" created a new event instead of updating the existing one.
  - **Requirement:** every `create` or `move` call passes two checks first — a duplicate check (`findDuplicateEvent`) and a create-vs-move check (`findReschedulableSibling`), plus the idempotency probe guarding both.
  - **Rule:** neither check may be removed, weakened, or routed around to make a fix simpler — not even when it looks like it's in the way. A fix that seems to require removing one is `needs-owner-decision`, never a unilateral call.
- **M15 · Floating blocks — position carries no ranking.** A floating block's position within its valid range carries no ranking. Every slot in range is equally correct, and a rebalance move is never chained against whatever action triggered it. There is no correct spot to preserve or revert to: once a floating block sits anywhere inside its valid range, that placement is as right as any other. So a rebalance-triggered move never needs undo-chaining, precedence logic, or ordering machinery against the action that triggered it — including a non-revertible action like a cancellation. Log that the move happened (the activity row) and stop there.
- **M16 · Availability — busy is never a hard blocker.** An attendee's busy time is never a hard blocker — but the search finds genuinely-free first, and annotation is the fallback, not the default. When booking with a specific person, try for a slot free for everyone FIRST (M2's Layer 1) — that's the real goal. Only when no such slot exists, or the ask is already known to be constrained, does it drop to relaxed/annotated mode: surface the conflict, whoever is booking decides — the owner (M8, no approval needed) or a colleague booking around another colleague (same non-blocking floor, their call). Once annotated and the booker still says book it, book it — every time, no exception.
- **M17 · Conflict handling — meeting conflict vs. off-hours differ.** A meeting conflict and an off-hours booking are different problems — only one of them is soft. Another meeting in the way is a movable commitment: annotate, let the booker decide (M16). Booking someone outside their own reasonable working hours (e.g. 2am their time) is not the same kind of conflict and is never reached the same way — it doesn't get silently offered or annotated-through like a meeting conflict does. It happens only when explicitly, specifically requested by name ("book it at 8pm their time"), never as a side effect of overriding a busy annotation.
- **M18 · Audit trail — every move, revert, rejection logged.** Every move, revert, and rejection is logged and queryable, never silent. When something moves (a meeting, a floating block), that move is logged — M15 already does this for floating-block rebalances; this generalizes it to every move. When an autofix or a change is rejected, that rejection is recorded somewhere queryable, not just a flag nobody checks. Not hypothetical: a rejected floating-block move and a rejected dense-setting meeting move have each recurred live — the owner said no, and the same move fired again anyway. And a revert never reaches back through a chain into something already removed — reverting a revert of a deleted item fails cleanly or does nothing, never a dangling reference.
- **M19 · Calendar-health looks ahead and optimizes proactively — it never waits for a request.** It scans the calendar ahead of time, detects issues (missing categories, anything else fixable before it becomes a problem), and tries to resolve them on its own initiative. It has memory: it remembers what it already tried and what the owner's feedback was, so something he already rejected never gets re-suggested as if it were new — this is exactly what M18's "logged and queryable" rejection record exists to make possible.

3. **Fix at the chokepoint, deterministically** — a return value the model reacts to, a single validator, a code-owned resolution. If a prior layer patched this, *remove* it.
4. **Paper-trace to 100%** (W7), then report per the return contract.
